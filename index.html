How It Works

A Partnership

CVA works alongside local schools — not in place of them. School counselors help students and families determine whether a CVA course is the right fit and facilitate the enrollment process. Once enrolled, CVA provides the online instruction, highly qualified teachers, and Georgia/CCSD-aligned curriculum. The home school remains the student’s foundation, while CVA provides flexible options to support their academic path.

Virtual Solutions

Who CVA Serves

CVA gives students flexible options when a traditional schedule does not meet every need. Some students use CVA to access a course that may not fit into their local school schedule, while others use it to stay on track for graduation, make room for specialized programs, or balance school with outside commitments. CVA gives students another path for completing coursework when online learning is the right fit. Whether a student is a competitive athlete managing a demanding practice schedule, a working artist balancing professional commitments with academics, or simply someone who learns better outside a traditional classroom, CVA is designed to meet them where they are. Online learning at CVA is not a shortcut. It is a rigorous, structured alternative that holds students to the same high standards.

How do I enroll in a CVA course?
Enrollment in CVA courses is handled through your home school counselor. Students and families should contact their school counselor to discuss course options and determine whether a CVA course is the right fit. The counselor will initiate the enrollment process on the student’s behalf.
Will my CVA course grade appear on my transcript?
Yes. Your CVA course grade goes on your permanent academic record, just like any class taken at your home school. It counts toward your GPA and may affect graduation requirements, college admission, and athletic eligibility.
Are CVA courses easier than in-person classes?
No — and this is one of the most common misconceptions. CVA courses cover the same academic content and hold students to the same standards as face-to-face equivalents. Because students are navigating a new learning environment at the same time, online learning can feel more demanding for students who are not prepared for the self-directed nature of the work.
How much time should I expect to spend on a CVA course each week?
Students should expect to spend the equivalent of a regular class period — typically 60 to 90 minutes — on their CVA course each day that it is scheduled. The online format requires the same time commitment as an in-person course; it simply offers more flexibility in when and where that time is spent.
Who do I contact if I need help in my course?
Your CVA teacher is your first point of contact. Students should reach out directly by email or through the course messaging system. CVA teachers are responsive and committed to supporting student success. If you need additional help, your home school counselor can also connect you with the appropriate CVA staff member.
What if I have an IEP or 504 Plan?
Students with an IEP or 504 Plan are fully supported in CVA courses. Make sure your plan information is shared with CVA before your course begins so that appropriate accommodations can be put in place. Contact your home school counselor or special education coordinator to ensure this information is communicated to CVA staff.
Can I take a CVA course while also attending my home school?
Yes. Most CVA students remain enrolled at their home school and take one or more CVA courses to supplement their regular schedule. CVA is designed to work alongside a student’s existing coursework, not replace the home school experience.
What technology do I need to take a CVA course?
Students need a reliable internet connection and a device capable of running a modern web browser — typically a laptop or desktop computer is recommended for the best experience. Cobb County School District provides devices to students who need them. Contact your home school for more information about device support.
Are there deadlines I need to meet in my CVA course?
Yes. CVA courses are paced, meaning students are expected to complete assignments and assessments according to a set schedule. Missing deadlines can result in falling behind or receiving a failing grade. Students who need support with pacing should reach out to their CVA teacher as soon as possible — do not wait until the end of the term.
How are grades reported for CVA courses?
CVA course grades are reported through the same systems used by Cobb County School District and are visible to students and parents through the standard parent portal. Grades follow the same grading scale and are reported on the same schedule as other district courses.
What happens if I need to drop a CVA course?
Course drops must go through your home school counselor and follow Cobb County School District’s drop/add policies. Deadlines and consequences for dropping a course vary depending on when in the term the request is made. It is important to act quickly if a course is not the right fit — contact your counselor as soon as possible.
Does CVA offer Advanced Placement (AP) courses?
Yes. CVA offers a selection of Advanced Placement courses taught by highly qualified teachers. AP courses through CVA follow the same College Board curriculum and exam schedule as AP courses offered at Cobb County high schools. Talk to your counselor about which AP options are available through CVA each term.
